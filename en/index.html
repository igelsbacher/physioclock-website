Start step by step

Get started – step by step

Take your time. You do not need to learn every feature at once.

1. Choose simple mode

Choose Simple mode when you first open the app. New users then see the separate usage notice. Choosing a mode does not grant any optional permissions.

2. Choose a programme

Tap Choose a programme. Load one of your saved sets or open Templates A, B and C. Choose duration, body focus and exercise count. Your current exercise plan changes only when you apply the template.

3. Adjust exercises if needed

In Edit exercises, change names and durations, add exercises or remove them. Save under a new name creates your own copy. Enter a name that is not already in use, then Save. Cancelling the name dialog keeps your draft in the editor.

4. Open training and start

Open training shows the timer. Timing begins only when you press Start. The current exercise and remaining time appear in large text.

5. Pause, Next and End

Pause stops the timer; Resume continues it. Next skips the current exercise. End finishes the session after confirmation and records actual movement time. Optional exercises are offered at the end.

6. Find it again

Your saved copy is under Choose a programme. End a running or paused session before switching programmes or editing exercises.

Further settings

Help → Settings → Display mode opens the mode choice. High contrast stays active in simple mode; you can increase text size further. Larger iOS accessibility text is respected. Existing rounds, rests, side changes, optional exercises and signals are kept when switching.

On this website, use your browser’s zoom to enlarge text. All steps remain understandable without animations.

Understanding movement

Understanding movement

Regular movement supports health and wellbeing. Short active breaks count too. Start at a level that suits your abilities and build up gradually.

For persistent nonspecific low back pain, an individually tailored exercise programme may help. No single form of exercise is best for everyone. Physical, psychological and social factors can influence pain.

As general guidance, WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity per week for adults, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. This is not an individual therapy prescription.

PhysioClock supports planning and recording. It does not replace a medical assessment or personal treatment advice. Seek medical advice for new, persistent or worsening symptoms.

The ring shows progress towards your time goal, not medical treatment success. Wellbeing records are personal reports and do not establish an effect of training.

Controller and contact

Heinz Josef Igelsbacher is responsible for PhysioClock and its accompanying website. Privacy and developer contact: dev.igelsbacher@icloud.com. App support: iphysioclock@gmail.com.

Data and purposes

The app processes exercises, sets, training duration and history, goals, weekly plans, favourites, settings and reminder times to organise training and display progress. Optional wellbeing entries and notes provide a personal overview. They are not used to produce diagnoses or medical recommendations. No separate PhysioClock account is required.

Free text and wellbeing

Exercise names, set names and notes may contain personal or health-related information. Enter only what you need for your overview and avoid information about other people. Free text is stored like other app data and synchronised when sync is enabled; JSON exports may include it. Entering text does not consent to advertising, research or medical analysis.

Optional iCloud sync

When enabled, exercises, sets, history, wellbeing and notes, goals, weekly plans, favourites, training settings and reminder times are stored in your Apple Account’s private CloudKit database and synchronised across your devices. Change timestamps and a generated device identifier resolve conflicts. No separate PhysioClock server stores this data. Turning sync off stops app synchronisation but does not delete existing cloud data. iOS or iCloud device backups are separate and depend on your Apple settings.

Apple Health, notifications and displays

After separate permission, Apple Health is read only to display today’s steps. PhysioClock writes no Health data and does not include the step reading in its history, exports or app iCloud sync. Local notifications require device permission. Optional widgets use training minutes, time goal and update time locally. Live Activities show remaining time and status, which may appear on the lock screen. They contain no wellbeing notes or exercise names.

Retention, deletion and export

Local content remains until you delete it or remove the app and its local data; existing backups and cloud copies are separate. Manage entries in their respective views. “Delete all training data” removes training content but does not completely erase all settings, cloud data or backups. With sync enabled, deletions may reach other devices at the next successful sync. Also manage cloud data in iCloud settings. Secure and delete exported files separately. JSON import creates a new set and does not restore all data.

Website and voluntary support

The website uses no advertising or analytics trackers of its own and reads no app training data. GitHub Pages logs visitor IP addresses for security. External links open services with their own privacy information. Feedback opens an editable email draft with app version, build, iOS version and device type; training or Health data is not automatically attached. Support receives your message and sender address only when you send it yourself. Please do not send health data. Support messages are retained as needed to handle the request, maintain necessary documentation or comply with legal obligations.

Providers, international processing and security

Apple provides iCloud, Health and the device platform; GitHub hosts the website. Apple and GitHub describe international processing, including in the United States, and safeguards such as standard contractual clauses or applicable privacy frameworks in their privacy policies. PhysioClock does not offer a choice or guarantee of storage exclusively in Switzerland or the EU. Local data relies on device security; the app adds no end-to-end encryption of its own. Protect your devices, Apple Account and exported files.

Your rights and optional permissions

Depending on applicable law, rights include access, rectification, deletion, data portability and, where applicable, restriction, objection and complaints to a competent data protection authority. Contact the developer. The developer has no central copy of content held exclusively on your device or in your private Apple Account for handling your request. Disable optional features in the app or system settings; this does not automatically undo previous processing or remove existing copies. Where the GDPR applies, necessary service provision relies on contract performance, optional processing requiring consent relies on consent, and necessary support or security relies on legitimate interests or legal obligations, as applicable.

First launch and changes

On first launch, the app displays usage notes and a link to this statement. You accept the usage notes and acknowledge the privacy information. The app stores the acknowledgement and its time locally and does not include them in its iCloud sync. This does not enable optional features. The information remains available in Help & Information. Material changes are published here with a new date; previous versions remain linked.

Medical classification

Medical purpose and regulatory status

PhysioClock is intended to organise user-selected exercise sessions, time them and record time spent training.

What PhysioClock does not do

The app does not diagnose conditions, assess symptoms, choose personalised therapies or replace medical advice or physiotherapy care. Exercises are selected by users, ideally with professional guidance where appropriate.

Is PhysioClock a medical device?

Based on the currently described functionality, it appears more likely to be an exercise planning and timing tool without an independent medical intended purpose. However, no binding regulatory classification has been established. Actual features, intended purpose and all promotional claims must be considered. A disclaimer alone does not determine medical-device status.

Safe use

Only perform exercises that are suitable for you and, where appropriate, agreed with a qualified healthcare professional. Stop if you experience pain, dizziness or unusual symptoms and seek professional advice.

  1. Start and read the display

    Load your prepared set and tap Start. The large countdown shows the time left for the current exercise. Separate progress indicators show the exercise and the whole session.

  2. Pause and skip

    Pause stops the session temporarily; Resume continues it. Next skips the current exercise. Skipped time does not count as active training. Reset discards an unfinished session.

  3. Finish and review

    End finishes a started session and saves it as ended early if it is not complete. A normally completed session is also recorded. Open the monthly view using the calendar icon.

  4. Signals and screen

    Configure sound and vibration in the exercise editor. iOS notifications, silent mode and Focus can affect signals. On iPhone, landscape view is available when rotation is unlocked. Check signals before first use.

Try it yourself

Use a short test set: Start → Pause → Resume → End. Check the calendar afterwards. Delete the test record from history if you do not want to keep it.

What success looks like

The countdown stops while paused. After End, the started session appears in history; only active time counts.

Optional exercise

Mark optional exercises in the exercise editor. They follow the required part. Choose whether to start them or finish for today. Declining still completes the required part. Decision time does not count as training. Both progress bars have midpoint ticks and a two-second “Halfway” cue. The countdown turns white during the final 15 seconds of each exercise.

Set volume

100% is the standard signal. Adjust its level from −50% to +50%. System volume stays unchanged; Silent mode, Focus and iOS notification volume still apply.

04 · Your calculated time goal

Remaining: 15 min

Your goal in the app

Tap the goal ring on the main screen or open Help & Information → Set a training goal. Complete the four steps, review the calculated goal and save. Tap the ring again to edit it.

How it is calculated

4 sessions × 15 minutes = a 60-minute weekly goal. 45 active minutes ÷ 60 target minutes × 100 = 75%. The ring closes at 100%; additional time stays visible in minutes and percent.

What counts?

Active minutes count within the current calendar day or Monday–Sunday week, in local time. Pauses and skipped time do not count. Current activity is shown provisionally; End or normal completion saves it. Reset discards the unsaved session.

What about existing data?

Existing records in the current period count too. Older entries without activity intervals are assigned to their completion date. New entries handle day and week boundaries. Editing a goal recalculates the current display. Previous target settings are not archived.

Goals and backup

Your goal also syncs when iCloud sync is enabled. JSON export includes the goal; import still creates a new set and does not fully restore goals or history. Removing a goal keeps training history. Deleting training records reduces calculated goal progress.

Time is not a treatment outcome

Motivation helps you plan. Percentages measure time only, not changes in symptoms, strength or mobility. Agree suitable exercises, duration and frequency with your healthcare professional.

Goal colours & app guide

Red means below 50%, yellow at least 50% but below 100%, and green at least 100% of your chosen time goal. Unrounded times determine the colour. Colours do not indicate medical treatment success.

With a daily goal, past days and today are compared with that daily target. Zero minutes is below 50%. Today’s result is provisional.

A weekly goal covers Monday to Sunday. The coloured weekly row includes the full week, even across month boundaries. Individual days remain neutral and show their minutes. The current week is still in progress.

Without a goal the calendar and ring stay neutral. Future days and weeks are not rated.

All periods are compared with your current goal. Changing your goal also changes colours for earlier periods.

Example entries

A check-in that suits you

Open Record wellbeing on the main screen. Choose one of five labelled options; none is preselected. Add an optional note of up to 300 characters and adjust the date and time. Save creates the entry; Later closes the form without saving.

After training or at any time

After a saved session, the main screen offers a check-in card. There is no mandatory pop-up. You can skip it or add an entry manually without training. Post-training entries can be linked to a session.

Chart, calendar and list

In Wellbeing & history, choose 7, 30 or 90 days. The chart and calendar show the last entry of each day. Days without entries have no point. Lines only connect existing entries. Select a day in the monthly calendar to list all entries from that day.

Edit and delete

Use an entry’s menu to edit its time, choice or note, or delete it after confirmation. The display recalculates. Deleting a training session does not automatically remove a separately saved wellbeing entry.

Personal data

Entries and notes are stored locally. With iCloud enabled, they also sync in your Apple Account’s private database. JSON export includes these entries. The current importer creates a new training set and does not restore wellbeing history. Delete all training data also removes wellbeing entries and notes.

Not a measure of training effectiveness

These entries are subjective snapshots. They change neither active time nor the goal ring. They cannot demonstrate a medical effect of training. The five categories are not combined into a clinical score or outcome prediction.

04 / 06 · About 7 minutes · Import, backup and iCloud

Take your data with you

  1. Import a new set

    Download a template below and save it in Files. In the app, open Help & Information → Backup & Deletion → Import as new set (CSV / JSON). Choose the file, name the set and confirm Add set. Existing sets, history and the active plan stay in place.

  2. Export a backup

    Under Backup, choose Export backup. Save the JSON file somewhere protected. It contains the current plan, sets, custom catalogue exercises and history, but not every app setting. The current importer creates a set from it; it does not restore all history.

  3. Enable iCloud

    Use the same Apple Account on iPhone and iPad and enable iCloud Drive. Open Reminders & iCloud on both devices and enable iCloud sync. Tap Sync now and read the status. This requires the app’s CloudKit service to be configured correctly.

  4. Understand sync

    Sync runs on opening and periodically in the foreground. Offline changes stay local and transfer later. A running timer does not transfer; sync waits during running or paused training. The latest change wins when the same entry is edited on both devices. Avoid editing the same set in parallel.

  5. Disabling is different from deleting

    Disabling sync does not delete cloud data. Deleting synced entries may affect the other device too. Export important data first. To remove all cloud data, disable sync on every device and use iCloud storage management. A device still syncing could upload data again.

Try it yourself

Import the JSON example as Practice. Check that your existing sets remain. Optionally create a test set on device A, sync both open apps and find it on device B.

What success looks like

The import appears as an additional set. During the optional iCloud test, the test set also appears on device B and the status reports a successful sync.

Understanding movement

Understanding movement

Regular movement supports health and wellbeing. Short active breaks count too. Start at a level that suits your abilities and build up gradually.

For persistent nonspecific low back pain, an individually tailored exercise programme may help. No single form of exercise is best for everyone. Physical, psychological and social factors can influence pain.

As general guidance, WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity per week for adults, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. This is not an individual therapy prescription.

PhysioClock supports planning and recording. It does not replace a medical assessment or personal treatment advice. Seek medical advice for new, persistent or worsening symptoms.

The ring shows progress towards your time goal, not medical treatment success. Wellbeing records are personal reports and do not establish an effect of training.

Controller and contact

Heinz Josef Igelsbacher is responsible for PhysioClock and its accompanying website. Privacy and developer contact: dev.igelsbacher@icloud.com. App support: iphysioclock@gmail.com.

Data and purposes

The app processes exercises, sets, training duration and history, goals, weekly plans, favourites, settings and reminder times to organise training and display progress. Optional wellbeing entries and notes provide a personal overview. They are not used to produce diagnoses or medical recommendations. No separate PhysioClock account is required.

Free text and wellbeing

Exercise names, set names and notes may contain personal or health-related information. Enter only what you need for your overview and avoid information about other people. Free text is stored like other app data and synchronised when sync is enabled; JSON exports may include it. Entering text does not consent to advertising, research or medical analysis.

Optional iCloud sync

When enabled, exercises, sets, history, wellbeing and notes, goals, weekly plans, favourites, training settings and reminder times are stored in your Apple Account’s private CloudKit database and synchronised across your devices. Change timestamps and a generated device identifier resolve conflicts. No separate PhysioClock server stores this data. Turning sync off stops app synchronisation but does not delete existing cloud data. iOS or iCloud device backups are separate and depend on your Apple settings.

Apple Health, notifications and displays

After separate permission, Apple Health is read only to display today’s steps. PhysioClock writes no Health data and does not include the step reading in its history, exports or app iCloud sync. Local notifications require device permission. Optional widgets use training minutes, time goal and update time locally. Live Activities show remaining time and status, which may appear on the lock screen. They contain no wellbeing notes or exercise names.

Retention, deletion and export

Local content remains until you delete it or remove the app and its local data; existing backups and cloud copies are separate. Manage entries in their respective views. “Delete all training data” removes training content but does not completely erase all settings, cloud data or backups. With sync enabled, deletions may reach other devices at the next successful sync. Also manage cloud data in iCloud settings. Secure and delete exported files separately. JSON import creates a new set and does not restore all data.

Website and voluntary support

The website uses no advertising or analytics trackers of its own and reads no app training data. GitHub Pages logs visitor IP addresses for security. External links open services with their own privacy information. Feedback opens an editable email draft with app version, build, iOS version and device type; training or Health data is not automatically attached. Support receives your message and sender address only when you send it yourself. Please do not send health data. Support messages are retained as needed to handle the request, maintain necessary documentation or comply with legal obligations.

Providers, international processing and security

Apple provides iCloud, Health and the device platform; GitHub hosts the website. Apple and GitHub describe international processing, including in the United States, and safeguards such as standard contractual clauses or applicable privacy frameworks in their privacy policies. PhysioClock does not offer a choice or guarantee of storage exclusively in Switzerland or the EU. Local data relies on device security; the app adds no end-to-end encryption of its own. Protect your devices, Apple Account and exported files.

Your rights and optional permissions

Depending on applicable law, rights include access, rectification, deletion, data portability and, where applicable, restriction, objection and complaints to a competent data protection authority. Contact the developer. The developer has no central copy of content held exclusively on your device or in your private Apple Account for handling your request. Disable optional features in the app or system settings; this does not automatically undo previous processing or remove existing copies. Where the GDPR applies, necessary service provision relies on contract performance, optional processing requiring consent relies on consent, and necessary support or security relies on legitimate interests or legal obligations, as applicable.

First launch and changes

On first launch, the app displays usage notes and a link to this statement. You accept the usage notes and acknowledge the privacy information. The app stores the acknowledgement and its time locally and does not include them in its iCloud sync. This does not enable optional features. The information remains available in Help & Information. Medical purpose and regulatory status

PhysioClock is intended to organise user-selected exercise sessions, time them and record time spent training.

What PhysioClock does not do

The app does not diagnose conditions, assess symptoms, choose personalised therapies or replace medical advice or physiotherapy care. Exercises are selected by users, ideally with professional guidance where appropriate.

Is PhysioClock a medical device?

Based on the currently described functionality, it appears more likely to be an exercise planning and timing tool without an independent medical intended purpose. However, no binding regulatory classification has been established. Actual features, intended purpose and all promotional claims must be considered. A disclaimer alone does not determine medical-device status.

Safe use

Only perform exercises that are suitable for you and, where appropriate, agreed with a qualified healthcare professional. Stop if you experience pain, dizziness or unusual symptoms and seek professional advice.
